# Hebrew style for all `he` text (used by p4c-translate.md)

Readers are bnei Torah and Chassidim who read Hebrew: yeshiva and kollel people, readers of contemporary sifrei shiurim and of the Torah pages of the Chareidi Hebrew press. Write the way a careful editor of a sefer of shiurim writes in Hebrew: clear, plain, flowing לשון הקודש of our times. It is not Israeli street or academic Hebrew, and it is not dense rabbinic Aramaic.

## 1. Register

- Plain present-day Torah Hebrew: `המשגיח מבאר ש...`, `הוא מקשה: ...`, `התירוץ הוא ש...`, `הוא מביא ראיה מ...`, `לפי זה`, `ממילא`, `דהיינו`, `כלומר`, `אמנם`, `ברם`, `על כן`.
- Learning vocabulary as it is used in the beis medrash: `קושיא`, `תירוץ`, `חידוש`, `פשט`, `נקודה`, `יסוד`, `ענין`, `עבודת ה'`, `יראת שמים`, `מדות`, `השגחה`.
- No Israeli colloquialisms or modern jargon (`בעצם` as filler, `כאילו`, `סבבה`, `אחלה`, `תכלס`, `ממש` as an intensifier where the source has no such word, `רלוונטי`, `פוקוס`, `מוטיבציה`, `אנרגיה`). No academic voice (`הדובר טוען`, `המחבר מנתח`, `ניתן לראות`, `יש לציין כי`).
- No Yiddish words or syntax carried over from the source. `ער זאגט אז` is `הוא אומר ש...`, not a Hebrew calque of Yiddish word order. Yiddish expressions that a Hebrew reader would not know are translated by meaning; a well-known one (`א גוטן שבת`, a niggun's name) may stay, in Hebrew letters, as in the source.

## 2. Spelling

- No nikud and no cantillation, ever.
- כתיב מלא as in Chareidi Hebrew print (`מסביר`, `שואל`, `אומר`, `כוונה`, `תפילה`), with loshon-hakodesh terms in their sefer spelling: `שבת`, `אמונה`, `צדיק`, `תשובה`, `רבונו של עולם`, `נפש האלקית`, `הקב"ה`, `השם יתברך`, `חז"ל`, `רש"י`, `גמרא`, `זוהר`.
- The Divine name as Torah print writes it in prose: `ה'`, `השם`, `הקב"ה`, `השי"ת`, `אלקים` / `אלוקים` (never the full Name, never `אלהים` written out in prose).
- Abbreviations with the ASCII double quote as gershayim (`חז"ל`, `רש"י`, `זצ"ל`, `ע"ה`) and the ASCII apostrophe as geresh (`ה'`, `ר'`), exactly as the Yiddish files do.
- Names of seforim, tzaddikim, places and parshiyos as the source spells them in Hebrew letters (`בני יששכר`, `רבי אלימלך מליזענסק`, `קארלין`, `פרשת נח`). Ashkenazi names stay Ashkenazi (`ליזענסק`, not `לז'ייסק`).
- Numbers and dates as the source has them: Hebrew years in letters (`תשמ"ט`), counts in words or digits as Hebrew prose normally writes them.

## 3. The Mashgiach

- He is `המשגיח`. Possessive: `של המשגיח`, `דברי המשגיח`. In a formal place (a title, a first mention where the source has his full name): `הגה"צ רבי משה וולפסון זצ"ל`.
- Never `הרב` as a stand-in for him, and never `שליט"א`: he has passed away, so `זצ"ל`.
- Other people keep their titles as in the source (`הרב מסאנז`, `רבי חיים`, `הבעל שם טוב`).

## 4. Quotations

Pesukim, Chazal, Zohar, Tanya and every other quoted source stay word for word as the source text quotes them, in their own language (Hebrew or Aramaic), inside quotation marks. Do not modernize or paraphrase a quotation; translate only the surrounding prose.

## 5. What is never written

- Anything about the audio's transcription, or any judgment of the shiur's clarity (`תמלול`, `משובש`, `לא ברור מה הוא אומר`, `קשה להבין`). A plain remark on the sound or the recording is allowed when the source has one (`ההקלטה נקטעת כאן`, `איכות הקול ירודה`).
- Anything the source does not say. No added explanations, sources, names, praise or conclusions.

## 6. Self-check

Before writing each `he` field, reread it once: would an editor of a Hebrew sefer of shiurim print this sentence as is? Nikud, a Yiddish word (`נישט`, `וואס`, `איז`, `אזוי`), an Israeli colloquialism, an English word in Hebrew letters, `הרב` for the Mashgiach, or a sentence the Yiddish does not support means: rewrite it.
